First-week checklist

Days 1 to 7: safety and rhythm before tricks or a busy social calendar.

  • Day 1: toilet trip as soon as you arrive home
  • Day 1: one quiet room only (no house tour yet)
  • Day 1: show bed, water, food, toilet spot, crate or playpen
  • Day 1: no welcome-party visitors
  • Day 1: let the puppy sleep when they need to
  • Days 2 to 3: same daily rhythm (toilet, meals, short play, naps)
  • House training: out after waking, eating, drinking, play, before bed
  • Praise the moment they finish outside (tiny treat is fine)
  • Accidents: enzyme cleaner, no punishment, keep the routine
  • Days 4 to 7: 30 to 90 second training sessions only
  • Practise name, come, sit, and leave it with treats
  • Daily handling: paws, ears, face, mouth, belly (seconds each)
  • Start alone time in seconds, then minutes (calm leave and return)
  • Crate stays a safe bed, never a penalty box

Shopping checklist

Before day 1, if you can. Your breeder or vet may adjust food and prevention.

  • Puppy food (breeder or vet recommended)
  • Food and water bowls
  • Comfortable bed
  • Puppy-safe crate or playpen
  • Harness and lightweight leash
  • ID tag
  • Puppy pads (if you plan to use them)
  • Poop bags and small training treats
  • Age-appropriate chew toys and soft toys
  • Puppy brush, comb, shampoo, nail clippers or grinder
  • Enzyme cleaner for accidents
  • Baby gates or barriers
  • Safe carrier or secured car harness
  • Puppy-proofing: cables hidden, small objects away, bins secured
  • Medications and cleaners out of reach; toxic plants checked

Night routine

Repeat nightly in the first weeks. Overtired puppies often get wild, not sleepy.

  • Last toilet trip outside (or pad) right before bed
  • Quiet room where the puppy can hear you nearby
  • Dim, calm evening: no rough play or new visitors
  • Water available; follow your vet or breeder feeding plan for late meals
  • Bed or crate set up as a safe place (door open at first if needed)
  • Short, boring goodbye if you step out of the room
  • If whining: toilet first, then calm reassurance, avoid a party at 2 a.m.
  • Track sleep: sudden bitey chaos may mean nap time, not more play

Grooming starter routine

First month: teach that touch is normal. You are not doing a full groom yet.

  • Once daily (or most days): 2 to 5 calm minutes total
  • Touch each paw and hold briefly; treat calm stillness
  • Lift ears gently; glance inside; treat
  • Touch muzzle and lips; treat
  • Run brush or comb through one small section; treat
  • Practice standing on a stable surface (table or mat)
  • Stop while the puppy is still relaxed (end on success)
  • Never force through fear; shorten and try again tomorrow

Vet-visit checklist

Bring paperwork. Ask questions. Call sooner if your puppy is weak or will not eat.

  • Breeder health and vaccination records
  • Microchip paperwork (if already placed)
  • List of food, treats, and any supplements you use
  • Questions about vaccination schedule and local disease risk
  • Parasite prevention plan (fleas, worms, etc.)
  • Weight check and feeding guidance for toy size
  • Dental care, grooming, and nail trimming advice
  • Neuter or spay timing (when appropriate)
  • Emergency clinic number saved in your phone
  • Discuss: repeated vomiting, diarrhoea, blood, breathing trouble, collapse, seizures, swollen painful belly, refusal to eat with weakness
